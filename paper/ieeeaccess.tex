\begin{abstract}
A classifier on a redundant detector array can fail in four ways: a known source, an unseen class, a detector glitch, or silent corruption of its arithmetic. Open-set scores and range checks read activation magnitude, so a new physical class and a flipped weight can raise the same alarm. This paper specifies a four-symbol isolability dictionary, known, novel, sensor, and compute, and four residuals that are each blind to at least one symbol. A physics residual compares detectors and does not read the weights. A checksum of the linear head does not read the sky. Morphology on the shared profile cannot be invented by a corrupt head. The lookup reads the checksum first. Five Fermi Gamma-ray Burst Monitor simulator seeds give 90.0\% four-way accuracy against 43.1\% for a range check. Held-out flashes are novel in 95.0\% of trials and a compute fault in 5.0\%. A rounding bound removes that leak at 32-bit precision. A labeled fusion of the same residuals reaches 96.5\% and usually calls a spike plus a bit-flip a sensor fault, where the lookup calls it compute. On 100 public continuous-time triggers, bit-flips stay isolated and no flash is called novel. A second draw keeps both results. Sensor isolation moves from 65\% to 7.5\% with direction. A Poisson likelihood ratio on one detector, amplitude maximized and with no detector response, raises those two rates to 100\% and 55\%. The same bit calls 20.6\% of simulator flashes a sensor fault. At one millisecond, 23 of 24 flashes fall outside the burst morphology, and the lookup calls them sensor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A classifier on a detector array can need four different responses to one unusual activation: accept a known event, preserve a possible discovery, isolate a bad sensor, or recompute a flipped weight. Treating those outcomes as one anomaly is the error this paper is about. Adapting a model to a bit-flip poisons a good network. Discarding a new class as a glitch throws away the event.

The Fermi Gamma-ray Burst Monitor (GBM) makes the four causes concrete. Twelve sodium iodide (NaI) detectors and two bismuth germanate (BGO) detectors observe the unocculted sky. Catalogued triggers include gamma-ray bursts (GRBs), solar flares, magnetar bursts (SGRs), terrestrial gamma-ray flashes (TGFs), and local and distant particle events~\cite{meegan2009,vonkienlin2020,bhat2016,roberts2018,briggs2013}. Public classifiers sort known classes and flag outliers~\cite{john2025}. Ground pipelines assume correct arithmetic. Onboard inference cannot. Silent data corruption is documented in production fleets~\cite{dixit2021,hochschild2021,dixit2022}, and soft errors can reach neural-network outputs~\cite{li2017,reagen2018}.

Open-set scores and range checks still collapse a new class and a bit-flip into one alarm~\cite{gavarini2022,progip2025,drdna2024,geissler2023,react2021}. Classical isolability has no symbol for a new class~\cite{gertler1990,venkatasubramanian2003,isermann2005}. Fermi pipelines veto single-detector spikes without an execution checksum~\cite{paciesas2012,goldstein2019,perera2025}.

Attribution by Residual-Invariance Analysis (ARIA) is a four-symbol dictionary, known, novel, sensor, and compute, read from residuals that are each blind to at least one symbol. The five-seed tables use a cosine-response simulator. Section~\ref{sec:real} repeats the lookup on \RealCubes{} public continuous-time (CTIME) cubes. Section~\ref{sec:tte} scores time-tagged event (TTE) light curves. Section~\ref{sec:extra} reports the splits, the empty novel symbol, the spike scores, and the compound faults. Software bit-flips are not a beam test.

Open-set scores reduce a surprising input to a reject bit: maximum softmax probability~\cite{hendrycks2017}, the energy score~\cite{liu2020}, ODIN~\cite{liang2018}, the Mahalanobis score~\cite{lee2018}, generalized ODIN~\cite{hsu2020}, and virtual-logit matching~\cite{wang2022}. OpenMax~\cite{bendale2016}, the line that begins with Scheirer and colleagues~\cite{scheirer2013,geng2021}, and a strong closed-set classifier~\cite{vaze2022} add an unknown class. Gavarini and colleagues then treat those bits as fault detectors~\cite{gavarini2022}. Selective classification still accepts or rejects and does not name a compute fault~\cite{sirc2022,narasimhan2024}. Anomaly surveys and OpenOOD compare detectors inside that one class~\cite{ruff2021,yang2022}. ProGIP separates in-distribution inputs, out-of-distribution inputs, and high-order bit-flips with range checks on gradients~\cite{progip2025}. ReAct showed that out-of-distribution inputs already occupy that extreme range~\cite{react2021}.

Dr.~DNA, quantile monitors, FACER, and BLINK flag silent corruption from activations or batch-normalization statistics, without a new-class symbol~\cite{drdna2024,geissler2023,schorn2020,blink2026}. Fault injection measures how often a flip changes an output~\cite{li2017,reagen2018,pytorchfi2020}. Structured parity residuals are insensitive to one fault and sensitive to another~\cite{gertler1990,venkatasubramanian2003,isermann2005,chen1999}. Algorithm-based fault tolerance detects a matrix-multiply error independently of the input~\cite{huang1984,zhao2021}. Intensity-guided and approximate checksums decide where to check and do not name discovery~\cite{kosaian2021,approxabft2023}. The rounding bound below uses the standard model of floating-point error~\cite{higham2002,ieee754}. On GBM, flight software requires a rate increase in two or more NaI detectors~\cite{meegan2009,paciesas2012}, ground localization compares detector rates~\cite{goldstein2020,connaughton2015}, and a Poisson matched filter searches every detector~\cite{perera2025}. Convolutional-recurrent classifiers flag outliers outside the trained classes~\cite{john2025}. Onboard learning assumes correct execution~\cite{bulgarelli2025}. Duration and spectral catalogs supply the morphology the novel symbol is trying to see~\cite{kouveliotou1993,poolakkil2021,vonkienlin2020}. No row of Table~\ref{tab:compare} puts known, novel, sensor, and compute in one dictionary whose residuals cannot see the wrong cause.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Problem}

Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times T}$ be background-subtracted counts from $D=8$ detectors and $T=64$ time bins. A linear head $f_W$ maps a morphology feature vector $\phi(X)$ to three known classes, GRB, SGR, and solar flare. TGF is held out. A single cause $c$ is drawn from
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{C}=\{&\texttt{known-clean},\ \texttt{novel-clean},\\
&\texttt{sensor},\ \texttt{compute}\}.
\end{align*}
The operational map is accept, preserve, isolate-channel, or recompute.

The required decision $\hat{c}(X,W)$ is uniquely determined, under a single-fault assumption, by a binary signature that does not use a learned fusion of OOD scores. Compound faults are not forced into $\mathcal{C}$.

\subsection{Physics residual, blind to the weights}

A genuine point source lights several NaI-like detectors with a shared time profile scaled by a cosine angular response~\cite{meegan2009}. Let $v$ be the median light curve of detectors whose total counts exceed a brightness gate, and let $r_{\mathrm{phys}}$ be one minus the leave-one-out cosine of the worst detector against $v$. Dropping that detector yields a gain $U_{\mathrm{det}}$. A localized spike raises $r_{\mathrm{phys}}$ and $U_{\mathrm{det}}$. A TGF that is still a point source does not. The median, rather than a principal component, is deliberate: a single loud spike must not become the template.

\subsection{Morphology residual, blind to a corrupt head}

The vector $\phi(X)$ concatenates duration, peak time, full width at half maximum, variability, hardness, log counts, concentration, and smoothness of the shared profile, not of $f_W(X)$. Duration and width follow the long-standing split of bursts by their time profile~\cite{kouveliotou1993}. Hardness uses the soft and hard counts after the worst detector has been dropped when the physics residual is already large. The residual $r_{\mathrm{morph}}$ is Euclidean distance to the nearest known-class centroid in that feature space, using centroids of the clean head. A flipped live weight cannot move $r_{\mathrm{morph}}$ except through $\phi$, which does not read $W$.

\subsection{Arithmetic residual, blind to the sky}

Let $W$ be stored at compile time with row-sum checksum $s^\top=1^\top W$~\cite{zhao2021,huang1984}. On a live inference the normalized residual is
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{exec}}=\frac{\lvert 1^\top(Wx)-s^\top x\rvert}{\lvert 1^\top(Wx)\rvert+\lvert s^\top x\rvert+1}.
\label{eq:exec}
\end{equation}
In exact arithmetic a new class cannot fire $r_{\mathrm{exec}}$, because $1^\top(Wx)=s^\top x$ for every input $x$. A weight bit-flip can. In finite precision both sums carry rounding noise that grows with the magnitude of $x$, so an input far from the calibration range can raise $r_{\mathrm{exec}}$ without any fault~\cite{higham2002}.

Under the standard model $\mathrm{fl}(a\circ b)=(a\circ b)(1+\delta)$ with $\lvert\delta\rvert\le u$, the standardized input $x$ is shared by the live product and the stored-checksum product, so its own rounding cancels. The fault-free absolute discrepancy then satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\lvert 1^\top(Wx)-s^\top x\rvert &\le B(x)=\gamma_N(u)\Big(\sum_j\big(a_j+\lvert s_j\rvert\big)\lvert x_j\rvert\\
&\quad+\sum_i\lvert b_i\rvert+\Big\lvert\sum_i b_i\Big\rvert\Big)+\eta,
\end{split}
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $a_j=\sum_i\lvert W_{ij}\rvert$, $\gamma_N=Nu/(1-Nu)$, $u$ is the unit roundoff, $N=20$ counts the longest chain of rounded operations plus a margin, and $\eta$ is an underflow term. The bounded rule fires when the absolute discrepancy exceeds $B(x)$. The vectors $a$, $s$, and $\sum_i\lvert b_i\rvert$ are stored with $s$ and are not recomputed from the live weights. $N=20$ was fixed before the bounded rule was evaluated.

\subsection{Signature and lookup}

Thresholds are set on clean calibration residuals only. No injected fault is used to set any threshold. The physics threshold is the 96th percentile of clean residuals, times $1.25$, and clipped to $[0.38,0.62]$. The morphology threshold is the 95th percentile times $1.25$. The calibrated checksum threshold is the 99.5th percentile of clean $r_{\mathrm{exec}}$, times $1.25$, and floored at the unit roundoff of the inference arithmetic. The drop threshold is the constant $0.28$. The signature is
\begin{align*}
S_{\mathrm{phys}} &= [r_{\mathrm{phys}}>t_{\mathrm{phys}}],\\
S_{\mathrm{morph}} &= [r_{\mathrm{morph}}>t_{\mathrm{morph}}],\\
U_{\mathrm{det}} &= S_{\mathrm{phys}}\wedge(U_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{raw}}>t_{\mathrm{drop}}),\\
U_{\mathrm{arith}} &= [r_{\mathrm{exec}}>t_{\mathrm{exec}}].
\end{align*}
Table~\ref{tab:dict} is the dictionary. Figure~\ref{fig:method} is the same lookup as a diagram. The live rule is
\begin{equation}
\hat{c}=\begin{cases}
\texttt{compute} & \text{if }U_{\mathrm{arith}},\\
\texttt{sensor} & \text{else if }U_{\mathrm{det}}\vee S_{\mathrm{phys}},\\
\texttt{novel-clean} & \text{else if }S_{\mathrm{morph}},\\
\texttt{known-clean} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:lookup}
\end{equation}
Checksum first is deliberate: a corrupt head must not be allowed to mint a discovery.

Three optional bits are not part of~\eqref{eq:lookup}. A short-window spike score uses the worst detector's two-bin excess over the median of the others, scaled by the square root of that median, with the same 96th-percentile rule and drop fraction $0.28$. A Poisson likelihood ratio on one detector uses that same gate and is reported in Section~\ref{sec:poisson}. If the checksum and the sensor bit are both true, \eqref{eq:lookup} still says compute. An alternative label ``multiple'' does not change any single-fault row.

\subsection{Simulator}

Each trial draws a sky position, a family template (fast-rise exponential-decay for GRB and SGR, a wide Gaussian for a solar flare, a millisecond TGF), Poisson NaI-like counts with a cosine response, and optional faults. Sensor faults add a soft spike on a dim detector. Compute faults flip one IEEE-754 bit of a linear-head weight, mild bits 18--23 or critical bits 28--30, and revert afterward~\cite{pytorchfi2020,li2017,ieee754}. The head is a standardized linear classifier on $\phi(X)$, not the convolutional-recurrent network of John and colleagues~\cite{john2025}. Inference is emulated in binary32. Binary64 and binary16 are sensitivity checks. The physics residual never needs the head.

Class counts in Table~\ref{tab:cat} are from the HEASARC \texttt{fermigtrig} table on 17 September 2026. They justify holding TGF out as a frequent, morphologically distinct family~\cite{roberts2018,briggs2013}, and they record that the charged-particle triggers are not used as light curves. The simulator's single-detector spike only loosely mimics those particle events.

\subsection{Baselines}

Energy and maximum-softmax rejection follow the open-set-score-as-fault-detector idea~\cite{gavarini2022,hendrycks2017,liu2020}: an unusual score is an undifferentiated anomaly. The range check follows ProGIP's magnitude idea~\cite{progip2025}: an extreme logit magnitude is compute, a leftover unusual score is novel, and anything else is known. The range check has no sensor symbol.

\subsection{Protocols}

The headline simulator uses training seeds $\{7,19,31,43,61\}$, evaluation seed $123$, and $36$ trials in each cell of the three known classes clean, TGF clean, sensor on GRB and on SGR, and mild and critical compute on GRB. That is $288$ four-way trials per seed. Thresholds use a disjoint clean calibration stream. Ablations reuse the same signatures with subsets of~\eqref{eq:lookup}. Compound trials inject a spike and a bit-flip together ($n=80$ per training seed in the signature study; the headline count uses one trained head and $n=80$).

The headline real-data protocol trains and scores known classes on the same cubes. TGF is never in training. Sensor spikes and critical bit-flips are injected on the first $40$ known cubes in file order (\RealTrials{} trials): every GRB in the slice, $15$ SGRs, and no solar flare. The same pipeline is also reported on every known cube. Mild flips, bits 18--23, use those $40$ cubes. Five-fold cross-validation, repeated five times, and a stratified half-split in both directions share no trigger name across a fold. They are still splits of one slice of \RealCubes{} triggers, not a second catalog draw.

\subsection{Checksum calibration, precision, and the rounding bound}
\label{sec:bounded}

The checksum threshold is set on clean residuals only. With that rule, compute isolation is $100\%$ in binary32 and binary64, but the held-out TGF is named compute in $\NovelAsCompute\%$ of binary32 trials. A TGF lies far from the calibration range, so its rounding noise can exceed a threshold calibrated on known classes. The checksum is blind to the sky in exact arithmetic, and not exactly so in finite precision.

Table~\ref{tab:modes} compares the calibrated threshold with the bound~\eqref{eq:bound} on the same trials. At binary32 the bounded rule names no TGF a compute fault (\BndNovCompThirtyTwo\%), keeps compute isolation at \BndCompThirtyTwo\%, including mild flips, and reaches \BndAccThirtyTwo\% four-way accuracy against \CalAccThirtyTwo\% for the calibrated rule. Figure~\ref{fig:checkscatter} shows why. In the calibrated panel a share of the TGF trials sits above the dotted threshold. In the bounded panel every fault-free trial, TGF included, is below $1$, and every flip in the protocol is above it. Figure~\ref{fig:checkroc} shows that at binary32 both statistics rank every compute trial above every TGF trial, so the false-compute rate of the calibrated rule comes from where its threshold sits, not from the residual being unable to rank the two causes.

The guarantee has a price at binary16. Compute isolation falls to \BndCompSixteen\% for the bound against \CalCompSixteen\% for the calibrated rule, and mild flips are caught in \BndMildSixteen\% of trials against \CalMildSixteen\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:checkprec}). Figure~\ref{fig:checkbits} breaks detection down by the flipped bit. The lowest mantissa bits are missed by every variant, because they move a weight by less than the arithmetic's own noise. At binary32 the calibrated rule reaches high detection from bit~8 and the bounded rule from bit~12. The bounded rule trades sensitivity for a guarantee of no false alarm on fault-free inputs.

As a soundness check, $\BoundInputs$ fault-free random inputs per precision were drawn up to $\BoundMaxSigma$ standard deviations from the training mean. The largest ratio of residual to bound was $\BoundWorstRatio$, and none exceeded $1$. This checks the bound on this head. It is not a proof for other heads, and a fault in the stored vectors themselves is outside the model.

\subsection{Poisson likelihood ratio on one detector}
\label{sec:poisson}

The short-window score is a Gaussian excess against the other detectors. Perera and colleagues search the same instrument with a Poisson matched filter, folded through the detector response and a bank of spectral templates~\cite{perera2025}. For a flat template and similar backgrounds their fixed-amplitude statistic reduces to that Gaussian excess. These cubes do not contain a response matrix. A glitch amplitude is not a GRB detection limit, so the bit measured here maximizes the amplitude on the counts that are already stored.

For each detector, and for the soft channel and the hard channel separately, the background is the upper median of the first eight bins and the last eight bins. That is the same edge used to subtract background elsewhere, and it is at least $0.5$ counts so the logarithm is defined. On a bin with value $d$ and background $b$, the likelihood ratio for a non-negative excess is $d\log(d/b)-(d-b)$ when $d>b$, and zero otherwise. The detector score is the maximum, over start time, of that ratio on two adjacent soft bins plus two adjacent hard bins. The event score is the largest detector. The bit fires when the score exceeds the 96th percentile of clean known cubes on the training draw, times $1.25$, and dropping the winning detector reduces the score by more than $0.28$. The threshold is fit on the training draw only. If the lookup already says compute, the call stays compute. Otherwise a firing bit is read as sensor. The injected spike is added only to the soft channel, which is the channel this ratio sees.

Table~\ref{tab:poisson} and Fig.~\ref{fig:poisson} report every protocol. The lookup column matches the rate already given for that protocol. The split between $100\%$ and $55\%$ is the threshold. On draw~1 it is $743.9$. The median clean score is $39.4$ and the median spike score is $3278.6$, so every injected spike clears it. On draw~2 it is $3190.3$. The median clean score is $57.4$. The 96th percentile is set by a few clean magnetar bursts and flares that already have a large one-detector excess, and the median spike score on that draw is $3297.5$, so the threshold sits on the spikes. The bit fires on $52.5\%$ of them. The union with the lookup is the $55.0\%$ in the table. On every spiked trial in the table, the detector with the largest likelihood ratio is the injected detector.

On the simulator the same bit raises sensor isolation from $77.5 \pm 0.6\%$ to $99.4 \pm 1.1\%$ and accuracy from $90.0 \pm 0.6\%$ to $92.5 \pm 0.7\%$. It also fires on flashes. Novel isolation falls from $95.0 \pm 3.2\%$ to $74.4 \pm 2.1\%$, and $20.6 \pm 2.2\%$ of flashes are called sensor. A point source near one detector is brighter there. This ratio does not divide by a detector response, so that brightness can clear the drop-gain gate. The median flash score is $3249.9$, above the simulator threshold of $1569.9 \pm 185.1$. On the public cubes the same flashes do not clear the gate: the bit fires on none of the TGFs in either draw. Critical bit-flips remain compute on every trial. The bit is true on $5.0\%$ of the draw-2 compute injections and on $1.1 \pm 0.6\%$ of the simulator compute injections, and the checksum-first rule leaves those trials as compute.

\subsection{Lookup against a labeled fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

The lookup is a table. Table~\ref{tab:fusion} asks what a learned combiner does with the same four numbers. The combiner is a class-weighted softmax on the physics residual, the morphology residual, $\log_{10}$ of the checksum, and the drop gain. The logarithm is a monotone rescaling: the raw checksum spans about eight decades, and a linear model standardized on that raw value cannot see a separation that a threshold can see. Training uses a disjoint simulator stream and includes flashes labeled novel. The lookup's thresholds are still fit on clean known-class residuals only. Both are scored on the published evaluation stream.

On single faults the fusion is the more accurate rule: $96.5 \pm 0.3\%$ four-way accuracy against $\AriaAcc\%$, sensor isolation $96.7 \pm 0.7\%$ against $\SensorIso\%$, and no held-out flash called compute. Compute isolation is $98.6\%$ on every seed, against $100\%$ for the lookup. The fusion does not turn a bit-flip into a discovery. On the 80 compound trials it does drop the checksum-first priority. It returns sensor on $72.5 \pm 7.5\%$ of them and compute on $27.5 \pm 7.5\%$, and novel on none. The lookup returns compute on all of them, including the trials whose sensor bit is also true. The accuracy belongs to the fusion, which was allowed to see the four labels. The refusal to preserve a corrupt product belongs to the lookup.

\subsection{A checksum on a small convolutional head}
\label{sec:conv}

The checksum in~\eqref{eq:exec} reads the linear head. It does not read a filter that sits in front of that head. Table~\ref{tab:conv} and Fig.~\ref{fig:conv} put the same identity on a smaller network trained here: four filters of width $5$ over the eight background-subtracted detector traces, then a linear readout to GRB, SGR, and solar flare. There is no recurrence. This is not the convolutional-recurrent classifier of John and colleagues~\cite{john2025}. The conv checksum compares the sum of the four filter outputs with a convolution by the stored sum of those filters. In exact arithmetic the two sides agree for every input, including a flash and a spiked detector. A flipped kernel weight breaks the agreement. The readout keeps its own row-sum checksum, which does not contain the kernels.

Table~\ref{tab:conv} and Fig.~\ref{fig:conv} give the rates, including closed-set accuracy $57.2 \pm 1.2\%$ (simulator) and $42.9 \pm 0.5\%$ (CTIME). The readout checksum crosses its clean threshold on $21.7 \pm 4.4\%$ of simulator critical kernel flips and $16.0 \pm 5.1\%$ of CTIME ones. The exact-arithmetic form of that residual stays below $10^{-6}$, and no filter output is non-finite. Those trips are the binary32 leak in~\eqref{eq:exec}, not a checksum of the kernel.

\section{Discussion}

The tables and figures above are the measurement. ProGIP is the nearest range-space neighbor and has no sensor symbol~\cite{progip2025}. Structured parity residuals have no symbol for a new class~\cite{gertler1990}. Checksum-first names a spike plus a bit-flip as compute; a labeled fusion of the same residuals usually names it sensor (Table~\ref{tab:fusion}). The four-filter checksum is not the convolutional-recurrent network of John and colleagues~\cite{john2025}. The paper does not report a beam test, a streaming monitor, a martingale test~\cite{hindy2025}, a detector-response template bank, or a classification of catalog particle rows.

\section{Conclusions}

ARIA is a four-symbol lookup whose residuals are forbidden from seeing the wrong cause. The contribution is the dictionary that keeps discovery as a non-fault symbol.
